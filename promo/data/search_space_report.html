Canoe Search Space

What the space of possible hulls looks like under your saved settings: which shapes pass, which parameters matter, what you trade for stability, and what the fastest valid hull looks like.

Settings: the web UI's saved settings (race objective, three crews: 2 paddlers 340 lb, 4 paddlers 680 lb and 700 lb; GM ≥ 3.0 in, freeboard ≥ 6.5 in, 0.375 in wall, tested mixes). Seven free shape parameters, bow and stern tied (symmetric). Every shape below ran the full wall search (7 mold depths × 5 tested mixes) and was scored exactly as the shape search scores it.

Summary

    The shape of the valid region

    It is a thin, slightly curved slab, one piece and nearly convex, lying against the wide end of the beam range.

    Six of the seven parameters can sit anywhere in their range and still give a valid canoe. Beam is the exception: the valid shapes span only about 25.6–29.3 in of the 18–30 in range. The slab has two faces.

    Lower face, stability. The narrowest beam that still reaches GM 3.0 in, for given other parameters. The face tilts. Fine, hollow bows (high bow waterplane exponent) and long tapers lose stability, so they need more beam. V-ish sections, rocker and draft give a little stability back. A linear fit of the best reachable GM explains 94% of its variation (a quadratic one 99.8%), so this face is smooth and slightly curved.

    Upper face, the beam limit. The 30 in overall beam cap, measured at the gunwale after the 3° flare. A deeper mold flares wider, so the cap on waterline beam drops by about 0.1 in per inch of mold depth.

    Where the stability face climbs above the beam cap (finest bows combined with the longest tapers), the slab pinches shut. That corner of the box has no valid canoe at all.

    The optimal hull

    The real optimizer (200 samples + 12 refinement rounds, verified at 321 stations) on your settings, next to two variants: a looser stability gate (GM ≥ 1.5 in), and wider bounds (length to 22 ft, draft to 10 in, section to 1.2). The optimum sits on the stability face with GM exactly 3.00 in, and against the edge of the box in three parameters: length, draft and section are all at their maximums.

    In words: an 18 ft hull at the full allowed length, about 27.6 in at the waterline (28.4 in overall), with long tapered ends (each end's taper covers 49% of the length, so almost no straight midsection), a moderately full bow (waterplane exponent 0.67), fairly V-shaped sections, moderate rocker and the deepest allowed design draft. The wall is the thinnest you can cast (0.375 in) in the lightest tested mix (M2 B, 54.9 pcf), on a 15 in mold. That gives 103.4 lb and a weighted race time of 103.4 s. Knee punching governs at D/C 0.70, well under the 0.93 gate. Stability and freeboard are the gates that actually bind.

    How many dimensions matter

    About four for speed, and one (plus two corrections) for being valid.

    A smooth quadratic model of race time that keeps only the best k parameters explains the share of the variation plotted below. Across the whole box, bow fineness, taper length, beam and length carry 82%; draft brings it to 88%. Inside the valid region, beam stops mattering (every valid shape is wide) and rocker and draft take its place. Section shape adds under 1% anywhere. Stability is nearly one-dimensional: beam alone explains 86% of the best reachable GM.

    Trade-offs

    The main trade-off is stability against speed. To measure it directly, the optimizer was re-run with the GM gate at 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4 and 5 in, and a 1500-shape random sweep at each setting measured how much of the box stays valid.

    Recommendations

    1. Length is the cheapest speed you are not allowed. The optimum is pinned at 18 ft. If the rules and your mold allow it, 22 ft (with draft to 10 in) gives 100.2 s, 3.2 s (3%) faster, for 14 lb more hull.
    2. Keep GM at 3 in, or raise it to 4. Going from 3 to 4 in costs about 0.3 s, which is within noise, and dropping to 2 in only saves about 0.7 s. Stability is cheap in race time up to 4 in, though the valid region shrinks sharply.
    3. Spend design effort on bow shape, taper and beam. They decide both speed and validity. Section shape and rocker barely matter, so choose them for building and handling.
    4. Check search.density_pcf. With strength_model = "tested_mixes", every tested mix is tried and the 63–80 pcf range in your settings is ignored. Every valid design uses M2 B (54.9 pcf). If you meant to exclude light mixes, that setting does not do it today.
    5. Expect the optimum to sit on the GM gate. Small changes to the crew weights or the assumed crew CG (12 in) move the stability face directly, and with it the answer. Pin down crew weights and CG before you freeze a mold.

    Method and limits

    Data. 3000 Latin-hypercube shapes over the full bounds (seed 7); four 30 × 30 grids and seven 41-step slices around the best of them; 1140 convexity probes (600 midpoints between random valid pairs, 60 segments × 9 points); 7 optimizer runs across GM gates, 7 × 1500-shape sweeps for feasible volume, and one optimizer run with wider bounds. Collected with the explorer (jaguar serve → /search), which runs the shape search's own scoring on each shape.

    Validity is exact. A shape is valid when some wall in the search passes every gate, exactly as the optimizer decides. Race times and margins for invalid shapes come from the wall closest to passing (smallest relative shortfall). A failing wall's numbers may stop at its first failing seat sample, so they read slightly optimistic.

    Statistics. Significance is the drop in R² of a full quadratic response surface (36 terms) when a parameter is removed; the explained share uses greedy forward selection with the same model. Spearman rank correlations are in the table. Race times use paths planned once on the midpoint shape (as the shape search does). The optimizer's re-planned times differ by about 0.1 s.

    Not covered. Bow and stern were tied (your symmetric = true), so asymmetric hulls were not explored. The wall thickness is fixed at 0.375 in by your settings. The GM gate depends heavily on the assumed crew CG height, which was not varied.
